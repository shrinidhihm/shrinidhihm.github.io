Design and Fabrication of Quad-Plane for All Terrain Applications

This project involved designing and fabricating a fixed-wing vertical take-off and landing (VTOL) unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) called Quad-Plane for all-terrain operations. It combined quadcopter and fixed-wing features to enable extended flight time and range, with runway-independent take-off and landing. The design accommodated a 3 kg payload.

The work examined flight dynamics, including roll, pitch, and yaw. XFLR5 software analyzed aerodynamic performance. The study also covered transition from quadcopter to forward flight mode. Ducted propellers improved thrust efficiency, extending endurance and range.

Supervised by Dr. Ashok B C at the Department of Mechanical Engineering, Vidyavardhaka College of Engineering, Mysuru.

Overview

Unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), or drones, are widely used in military and civilian sectors. They operate without onboard humans.

The Quad-Plane merged quadcopter and airplane principles, using five propellers: four for vertical lift and one for forward thrust. The quadcopter setup had two pairs of motors rotating oppositely for balance. Ducted propellers channeled airflow to boost thrust efficiency and lift.

The front propeller drove horizontal flight after altitude gain.

Conclusion

The Quad-Plane was successfully designed, fabricated, and tested, demonstrating effective VTOL capabilities and efficient transition to forward flight. The use of ducted propellers enhanced thrust efficiency, extending flight time and range. Aerodynamic analysis via XFLR5 confirmed stable flight characteristics across various conditions.

This project addresses key challenges in all-terrain UAV applications, such as emergency supply delivery, with a cost-effective and robust design.
