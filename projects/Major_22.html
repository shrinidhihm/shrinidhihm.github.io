Design and Fabrication of Quad-Plane for All Terrain Applications

Led a team of four to design and fabricate a fixed-wing electric vertical take-off and landing (eVTOL) UAV with 1.1 m wingspan and 3 kg maximum take-off weight, optimized for medical supply delivery in all-terrain environments.

Conducted aerodynamic design in XFLR5, CAD modeling in SolidWorks, and CFD-based optimization with structural validation in Ansys Workbench. Designed and 3D-printed propeller ducts to improve propulsion efficiency and safety.

Supervised by Dr. Ashok B C at the Department of Mechanical Engineering, Vidyavardhaka College of Engineering, Mysuru.

Overview

The Quad-Plane is a hybrid fixed-wing VTOL UAV that combines the vertical take-off and landing capability of a quadcopter with the efficiency and range of conventional fixed-wing flight.

It uses four ducted propellers for vertical operations and a fifth forward-mounted propeller for cruise. Counter-rotating motor pairs provide stability in hover, while ducted shrouds improve thrust efficiency and propeller safety.

After gaining altitude in quad mode, the vehicle transitions to forward flight by tilting forward and engaging the pusher propeller, allowing longer endurance and greater range than pure multirotors.

The design targeted all-terrain applications, particularly emergency medical supply delivery, with emphasis on payload capacity, slow-flight controllability, and runway-independent operation.

Design Process

Initial sizing was performed in OpenVSP, followed by detailed aerodynamic evaluation in XFLR5. NACA 4415 was selected for the main wing due to its favorable lift-to-drag characteristics at the operating Reynolds numbers, while NACA 0015 was used for the tail surfaces.

A rectangular wing planform was chosen for optimal aerodynamic performance and ease of manufacturing. A conventional tail configuration provided longitudinal and directional stability while avoiding risks associated with alternative layouts.

Structural integrity of key components was verified through finite element analysis in Ansys Static Structural. Propeller ducts were designed and 3D-printed to enhance propulsion efficiency and safety.

Aerodynamic Analysis

XFLR5 was used to model the complete aircraft and analyze pressure distribution, flow streams, induced drag, and viscous drag at cruise speed across a range of angles of attack and control surface deflections.

Simulations confirmed stable flight characteristics and acceptable drag penalties during low-speed maneuvers with deployed ailerons and flaps.

Flight System

The system comprised a 3300 mAh LiPo battery, brushless DC motors, electronic speed controllers, metal-gear servos for control surfaces, and a Pixhawk flight controller running ArduPilot firmware.

Conclusion

The Quad-Plane was designed, fabricated, and tested successfully. It demonstrated effective VTOL capabilities and smooth transition to forward flight. Ducted propellers enhanced thrust efficiency, extending endurance and range. XFLR5 analysis confirmed aerodynamic stability across conditions.

The design addressed all-terrain UAV needs, such as emergency medical deliveries, through a robust and cost-effective solution.
