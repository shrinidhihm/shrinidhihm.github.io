Aerodynamic Design of a Fowler Flap for a Hybrid Laminar Flow Control Airfoil

Studienarbeit project focused on maximizing lift (CL,max) for a Fowler flap on an HLFC airfoil at 15° (takeoff) and 30° (landing) deflections. Conducted 1000+ CFD runs with manual OAT optimization, achieving 4.14% and 4.56% improvements in CL,max.

Supervised by Prof. Dr.-Ing. Ingo Staack and Dr.-Ing. Stanislav Karpuk at IFL, TU Braunschweig, as part of SE2A Cluster of Excellence. Issued Dec 2024, submitted April 2025.

Motivation

Driven by EU Flightpath 2050 goals for 75% CO2 reduction per passenger-km and 90% NOx cut. Global efforts, like ICAO's CORSIA and Airbus's zero-emission concepts (e.g., ZEROe), emphasize innovations in aerodynamics, engines, and fuels. High-lift devices like Fowler flaps are key, boosting maximum lift by up to 100% during low-speed phases (takeoff/landing), which directly cuts fuel use and emissions.

This project optimizes a Fowler flap on an HLFC airfoil to maximize lift while maintaining efficiency, addressing challenges like flow separation and drag. By integrating HLFC technologies (e.g., boundary-layer suction), it supports breakthrough designs for fuel-efficient aircraft. My work refines flap parameters to improve CL,max, contributing to safer, greener operations—aligning with industry focuses on advanced wing systems.

Methodology

Adapted the PyAeroSweep framework for fully automated CFD analysis of an HLFC airfoil equipped with a Fowler flap. The workflow integrates four key stages:

1. Geometry Definition

  • Employed PARSEC parameterization for the main airfoil, fixed 15° droop nose, and Fowler flap
  • Key flap design variables: chord ratio (cf/c = 0.3), horizontal/vertical gaps (x_gap, y_gap), leading-edge radius (r_le_flap), and deflection angles (δf = 15°/30°)
  • These parameters control slot geometry and flow attachment (baseline values in report Table 2.2)

2. Mesh Generation

  • Used Fidelity Pointwise to generate hybrid unstructured meshes (triangular core + T-Rex anisotropic prismatic layers near walls)
  • Targeted Y+ ≈ 1 for accurate boundary-layer resolution
  • Typical settings: 40 prismatic layers, growth rate 1.2, initial Δs calculated from Re and skin-friction coefficient

3. CFD Simulation

  • Steady RANS simulations with SU2 open-source solver
  • Turbulence model: k-ω SST (well-suited for adverse pressure gradients and separation on flaps)
  • Freestream conditions: Mach 0.21, sea-level altitude, AoA sweeps from -2° to stall
  • Convergence criteria: density residual drop, Cauchy criterion on CL (ε = 5×10-7), max 25,000 iterations

4. Post-Processing

  • Extracted aerodynamic coefficients (CL, CD, etc.) from SU2 .csv history files using MS Excel
  • Visualized flow fields (velocity contours, separation zones, pressure distributions) in ParaView from .vtu output

This automated pipeline enabled over 1,000 simulations while maintaining a balance between computational cost and numerical accuracy—essential for practical parametric optimization studies.

Mesh Sensitivity

Tested coarse (≈84k cells), baseline (≈112k cells), and fine (≈138k cells) meshes at 15° flap deflection. The baseline mesh was selected due to only a 0.27% difference in CL,max from the fine mesh, while offering significantly lower computational cost—essential for conducting over 1,000 parametric runs in the optimization study.

Parameter Sensitivity

Used One-At-A-Time (OAT) optimization: Varied 10 parameters sequentially, holding others fixed, across AoA sweeps to maximize CL. Most sensitive: x_gap (optimized to 0.75% chord for better slot flow) and y_gap (1.97% for delayed separation). Less sensitive: shroud lip extent. Iterated until convergence, revealing trade-offs like increased gap reducing drag but risking early stall.

Key Improvements

CL,max increased by 4.14% at 15° deflection (takeoff) and 4.56% at 30° (landing). Better L/D at low α; reduced separation via optimized gaps/shapes. Baseline vs. optimized: Larger y_gap enhances energized flow over flap, delaying stall by 1-2° AoA.

Figure 4.1: Baseline (red) and optimized (blue) airfoil/flap profiles at 15° and 30° deflections, showing reshaped shroud and increased gaps for better performance.

Summary

1000+ CFD runs yielded optimized flap with cleaner channel flow, delayed separation, and higher CLmax. Foundation for URANS and automated (SBO) optimization in future work.
