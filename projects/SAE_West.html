SAE International - SAE Aero Design West 2022 – Van Nuys, California, USA

SAE Aero Design West 2022 was a defining milestone in my undergraduate journey. I had the opportunity to lead Team Vymanika as Captain for SAE International’s premier aero design competition, held in Van Nuys, California.

Our team designed, analyzed, fabricated, and prepared a radio-controlled aircraft under strict dimensional and performance constraints, while simultaneously managing the complexities of international travel, logistics, and competition readiness during the post-COVID period.

This experience remains one of the most transformative phases of my engineering development, combining technical depth with leadership, responsibility, and resilience.

Supervised by Prof. Arun C Dixit at the Department of Mechanical Engineering, Vidyavardhaka College of Engineering, Mysuru.

Overview

The SAE Aero Design Challenge 2021 aimed to design a radio-controlled aircraft capable of lifting the maximum possible weight while complying with specified power limits and aircraft dimensions (length, width, height).

Team SWIFT 7 focused on a monoplane configuration with a rectangular wing, flaps, aluminum fuselage for strength and weight reduction, balsa wood wings, and depron tail. The project emphasized real-world unmanned air vehicle requirements, balancing design process and communication skills.

Due to COVID-19, we adapted by using improvised materials like carton and aluminum rods for the initial prototype, successfully building and test-flying it despite resource constraints.

Fabrication and Testing

Due to the COVID-19 pandemic and associated restrictions, the physical competition was cancelled. However, the team successfully adapted by constructing and test-flying a functional prototype using readily available materials such as carton and aluminum tubes to validate the core design concepts, aerodynamics, and flight characteristics.

The intended final aircraft was designed to utilize aluminum for the fuselage, balsa wood for the wings, and depron sheets for the tail section. Selected propulsion components included the iFlight Xing 660KV motor and a 16x10E propeller.

Despite the inability to fabricate the full-scale aircraft with the planned materials, the prototype flights provided critical validation data. The team submitted comprehensive application, design, and fabrication reports, along with a virtual technical presentation, as part of the modified competition format.
